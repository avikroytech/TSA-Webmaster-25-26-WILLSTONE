# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Willstone serves three primary audiences:

- Residents who need to find services, events, maps, government information, guides, and local support without friction.
- Visitors and newcomers who need to understand the city, its districts, public resources, and civic identity quickly.
- TSA judges who need to see a polished, coherent, accessible civic website with credible information architecture and strong visual execution.

Residents are the highest frequency users. Their context is practical: they may be looking for a clinic, transit information, a public meeting, a local guide, or a way to submit a useful resource. Visitors and judges should still understand the city at a glance through confident storytelling, clear navigation, and a memorable official identity.

## Product Purpose

Willstone is the official digital home for a fictional four-island city. It brings city services, community resources, events, maps, news, civic guides, resident submissions, and the Beacon assistant into one accessible hub.

Success means a user can quickly understand what Willstone is, trust it as an official civic source, and complete common tasks such as finding a resource, exploring the map, checking events, learning about government, or submitting a resource. For judging, success also means the site feels intentional, complete, and distinctive rather than template-driven.

## Brand Personality

Municipal, helpful, modern, calm.

The voice should feel official without being cold, welcoming without being casual, and modern without drifting into startup language. Copy should be concise, civic-minded, and useful. The interface should signal public trust, local care, and organized access to information.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI slop: template landing-page sections, vague inspirational copy, identical card grids, ornamental gradients, excessive glow effects, glassmorphism as decoration, and generic SaaS aesthetics.

Avoid fantasy-game styling, overly whimsical city lore, cluttered government portals, and corporate startup visuals that make the city feel like a software product rather than a public civic place.

Avoid designs that hide practical services behind decorative storytelling. The brand can be expressive, but residents still need fast routes to services, maps, calendars, guides, and submissions.

## Design Principles

1. Make the city feel official before it feels promotional.
2. Put resident tasks within reach on every major surface.
3. Use the four-island identity as structure, not decoration.
4. Keep civic information calm, scannable, and trustworthy.
5. Let accessibility and clarity be part of the brand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA as the baseline. The site should support keyboard navigation, visible focus states, readable type sizes, sufficient contrast, meaningful labels, reduced motion, high contrast mode, and clear error messaging.

Accessibility is not an optional settings panel only. Core pages, forms, navigation, filters, maps, resource cards, and assistant entry points should remain usable without animation, with larger text, and with assistive technologies.
